01 — Key force

It hears how hard you hit.

MacBooks with a motion sensor feel each keystroke through the chassis. thock reads it ~800 times a second and plays firm hits louder and brighter, light taps softer and duller.

Adjustable from soft to hard — or off, if you want every key the same.

04 — Install

One extra step, once.

thock is a free project and not notarized by Apple, so the first launch takes one extra step on macOS 15 and later. After that it just runs.

  1. 01Download thock-<version>.dmg, open it and drag thock into Applications.
  2. 02Open thock. macOS will say it “was not opened”.
  3. 03Go to System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway next to thock and confirm with your password.
  4. 04thock starts and shows a short setup window. Follow it to allow Input Monitoring — that is how it sees your key presses. If macOS offers to quit and reopen thock, accept.
Prefer the Terminal? Skip steps 2–3.

Remove the quarantine flag after copying the app:

xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/thock.app

Or, once the Homebrew tap is published:

brew install --cask --no-quarantine obsiidi/thock/thock
05 — Requirements

macOS 13 Ventura or later, Apple Silicon or Intel.

Key force needs the built-in motion sensor: MacBook Pro or Air with M1 Pro/Max or any M2 or later chip, typing on the built-in keyboard. On other Macs and external keyboards thock plays at a fixed loudness and tells you so.

06 — Privacy

thock asks for Input Monitoring because that is the only way to hear key presses. It turns each press into a sound and nothing else: no keystrokes are stored, logged or transmitted.

The motion sensor is read for impact strength only. The app makes a single, optional network request — asking GitHub whether a newer release exists. No account, no analytics, no telemetry. Full privacy notice.
